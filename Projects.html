Senior Design - Automated Train Bearing Inspection Machine

For my senior design project, I had the oppurtunity to work with a team of 5 other student engineers on a project for Wabtec, a locomotive part manufacturer. We were tasked with building a machine that could take in a used locomotive bearing, measure its inner and outer diameter to within 0.0001" accuracy, then use lidar to scan the inner and outer surface for scratches and defects larger than 0.03". Finally the machine would mark the bearing as either "pass" or "fail" based on the measurements and send it down a gravity fed slide, where it would be unloaded by an operator.

The majority of the design work was already completed when my team starting working on this project. It was our job to essentually "get it working" As the Electromechanical engineer on the team, my responsibilities mainly involved integrating all of the electrical subsystems with the Programmable Logic Controller (PLC). This included wiring up motors, sensors, the lidar unit, and the human machine interface (HMI). I also wrote much of the PLC code using ladder logic to control the sequence of operations for the machine. Another part of the project I was heavily involved with was the mechanical design of the saftey cage that surrounded the machine. The cage was designed to keep operators safe from moving parts and to contain any debris that might be ejected from the machine during operation. The cage was built using standard 8020 extruded alluminum and 1/4" polycarbonate panels. I used Solidworks to design the cage
